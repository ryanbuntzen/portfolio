Laser

Laser plans my day for me. It acts as a Todoist client, utilizing the user's Todoist account but adding in new features I felt were personally useful. It pulls the user's calendar from Google, and fills the gaps with Todoist tasks. There are two focus modes: Tunnel vision shows the time-block the user is in and nothing else. Laser focus functions similarly, but features any uncompleted previous tasks as well.

Bruin Swipe

I wanted to track what I was eating in the dining halls without typing every plate into MyFitnessPal. This has every item from every hall, café and truck on campus. The user can add one to their day and it'll log the whole nutrient panel for that item. UCLA only publishes 19 nutrients, so the other 20 I had to work out by parsing the ingredient list, matching each ingredient against the USDA database, and then fitting how many grams of each the serving holds to the numbers UCLA publishes. Once you have logged something, it starts telling you what nutrients you are short on and the dining halls and foods that fill those gaps.

Skycraft

A Minecraft-esque flight simulator I quickly built for fun while I was in class. The island is randomly generated from noise when the page loads, and three.js creates the sky and the water. Controls: mouse for pitch and roll, A and D for rudder, W and S for throttle, shift to boost. Press C to change camera modes.

Dispatcher and daily brief

Nine time-based scheduled jobs underneath one dispatcher, dispatching every five minutes. It keeps its own schedule through a Todoist task in order to run from Laser. If my laptop was asleep when a task was meant to run, it runs it as soon as my laptop awakens. In the morning, it puts any events from my email into my calendar and sends a daily update to my phone on Telegram.

python, todoist api, google calendar, telegram

Content pipeline

I automated content creation for a YouTube channel I post to. After writing the script, Speechify converts it into a voice clip, ffmpeg lays b-roll against the voiceover, Remotion renders, and the YouTube API does the upload and the scheduling. About 2,300 lines of Python. Ten Claude Code skills work throughout the pipeline in a ten stage process.

python, remotion, ffmpeg, youtube api

Scholarship pipeline
